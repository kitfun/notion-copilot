# Privacy

Cofun makes AI side panels for Chrome, each one an extension for a different kind of page. The extensions share one sign-in and one server (the worker): this page says what they do with your data, the same for every extension. What each extension reads in your browser, which hosts it sends data to, and what it keeps on your device is on that extension's own privacy page; the two pages together are its privacy policy:

- [Notico](extensions/notico/PRIVACY.md), for Notion

This page describes the worker deployed from `worker/` and the extensions built from this repository with their committed configuration. If you build an extension against your own deployment, the hosts are the ones you configure.

The short version: when you ask a question, the conversation, including what the extension read from your pages to answer it, goes to our worker and from there to an AI model that runs on Cloudflare. We do not store that content on our servers. The worker keeps your credits and a record of what each request cost, never what you asked or what the AI answered.

## The sign-in page

Every Cofun extension signs you in on the same page: its "Sign in" opens `https://api.cofun.cc/login` in a normal tab. That page runs Clerk's sign-in code, loaded from Clerk's Frontend API (`clerk.cofun.cc`). What you enter there (an email address, or a Google sign-in) goes to Clerk and to the sign-in provider you choose, not to our worker. Clerk holds your account: your email address, name, and avatar as the sign-in provider gives them. See [Clerk's privacy policy](https://clerk.com/legal/privacy). An extension shows your name, email address, and avatar from that account in its side panel.

## Buying credits

You can buy credits with a one-time payment. The extension asks the worker for a checkout, and the worker asks [Stripe](https://stripe.com) to make one; the extension then opens Stripe's Checkout page (`checkout.stripe.com`) in a normal tab. Stripe sells the credits as the merchant of record, through its Managed Payments: everything you enter there (your email address, name, billing address, and payment details) goes to Stripe and to Link, Stripe's checkout and support service, not to our worker or the extension. See [Stripe's privacy policy](https://stripe.com/privacy).

- **What the worker sends to Stripe.** To make a checkout: your Clerk user id, the pack you chose (how many of Cofun's 1,000-credit units, and the text saying how many credits that gives), and the addresses of the pages Stripe sends you back to. Stripe keeps them with the payment.
- **What Stripe sends to the worker.** Stripe tells the worker's webhook when a payment goes through, is refunded, or is disputed. The worker reads from it only your Clerk user id, the pack you chose, whether the payment went through, and the payment's Stripe reference (its PaymentIntent id); to check what was paid for, it asks Stripe for the checkout's items (which price, and how many). It does not receive your payment details.
- **What the worker sends to PostHog.** When a payment adds credits, the worker tells [PostHog](https://posthog.com), which we use to see how the extensions are used, that a purchase was completed: your Clerk user id and the pack you chose (how many 1,000-credit units), and nothing else. It holds no amount, currency, or payment reference, nothing you entered at Stripe, and it tells PostHog not to work out a location from the request, which comes from the worker, not from your device. Each extension's privacy page says what usage data the extension itself sends to PostHog, and how to turn that off; this event comes from the worker, so that switch does not cover it.
- **What the worker keeps.** A purchase is a grant of credits like the others (below), with the Stripe reference of the payment it came from, and, if it was refunded or disputed, that it was taken back and when. A payment refunded or disputed before its credits were added is kept as its Stripe reference, whether it was a refund or a dispute, and when, with no user id, so that its credits are never added. It logs each purchase with your Clerk user id, the credits, and the payment's reference, and each purchase taken back with the payment's reference and why.

## What the worker does with it

The worker is the code in [`worker/`](worker/), running on Cloudflare Workers.

- **It checks who you are.** It verifies the session token with Clerk's public key, without calling Clerk, and uses only your Clerk user id from it. For the operators' admin page it also reads the token's `role`, which says whether the account is an operator's; for every other account it is empty.
- **It passes the conversation to the model and streams the reply back.** The model runs on Cloudflare's [Workers AI](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/), reached through Cloudflare's AI Gateway. The gateway's log collection and cache are turned off for these requests, so it does not keep your messages or the replies; it keeps request analytics (counts, tokens, cost) tagged with your Clerk user id and the extension the request came from. The worker itself does not store or log the conversation. Cloudflare processes it to run the model; see [Cloudflare's privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).
- **It keeps your credits.** Using the assistant costs credits, and every account gets free credits each week. The worker keeps them in a database on Cloudflare ([D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/)), by Clerk user id: each grant of credits you were given (how many, how many are left, when they were granted and when they expire, and whether they were the free weekly grant, credits an operator granted you by hand, with that operator's Clerk user id, or credits you bought, with the Stripe reference of the payment and whether it was taken back after a refund or a dispute), and one record for each call to the AI model your questions made, whether it was answered or failed: when it started and ended, which extension it came from, the model, whether the reply was streamed, how it ended and why the model stopped, the number of input tokens (with how many the model read from its cache) and output tokens (and whether they are an estimate, for a reply that was cut short), and the credits it cost. It holds numbers, times, the model's name, and those ids only, never what you asked or what the assistant answered. Grants and model calls are kept without an end date, after grants expire too, as the record of what the account was given, asked of the model, and spent. Operators of the service can look at an account's credits by its Clerk user id, and grant it credits, and can total grants and model calls across accounts and list accounts, by Clerk user id, with what each was granted and spent.
- **It shows you your own credits.** An extension asks the worker for the signed-in account's credits: how many are left, when they expire, and when free ones arrive; how many were used on each of the last 30 days; and, only when you open the history, every grant of credits you were given, the expired ones included (where it came from, how many, how many are left or went unspent, and the days it was given and expires or expired; never which operator granted it), and how many credits you used on each day, as far back as you have used the assistant. The worker works all of these out from the records above each time, for the signed-in account only, and keeps nothing new for it. So that a day is a day on your clock, the extension sends how far your clock is from UTC with that request; the worker uses the number for the answer and does not store it, though it is part of the request's address in Cloudflare's request logs (below), as is the date or the place in the list a request for older history goes on from.
- **It writes one usage record per chat request.** The record holds your Clerk user id, which extension the request came from, the model, whether the reply was streamed, the outcome, the finish reason, the number of messages, the size of the input in characters, the token counts (with how many of the input tokens the model read from its cache), the credits charged, and the duration. It never holds message or reply text. Records are kept in Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine, which deletes them after 90 days.
- **Cloudflare keeps the worker's logs.** These are the request logs Cloudflare records for every Worker (such as the time, URL, and status of a request) and the errors the worker prints when a request fails. The worker does not write the conversation to them. When a model call fails, before or partway through a reply, the credit ledger fails, or a request fails in a way the worker did not expect, it logs only the first 60 characters of the error's name and the first 200 characters of its message, never the whole error: if the model provider's error quoted part of a request, no more than that could reach the logs. When the usage record fails, it logs that error whole; it is never given the conversation. When an operator grants credits by hand, it logs the operator's Clerk user id, the account's Clerk user id, the number of credits, and when they expire. Purchases are logged as described under [Buying credits](#buying-credits).

## Changes and contact

This file changes in the same pull request as any change to what the worker or the sign-in receives, stores, or logs; its [history](https://github.com/cofun-labs/cofun/commits/main/PRIVACY.md) shows every change. Questions and reports: [open an issue](https://github.com/cofun-labs/cofun/issues).
